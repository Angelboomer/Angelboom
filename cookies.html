Cookies

Want a cookie? Too bad! I'm taking yours though. No really, I'm taking yours right now. Just kidding. But did you know
every time you open Amazon, or check out your cart in Temu, that they're storing your information in the form of cookies?

Website cookies are tiny text files saved on your device that remember your preferences and improve your browsing experience. They can be essential
(like keeping you logged in), functional (like saving your language settings), performance-based (like tracking page speed), or advertising-related
(like showing you relevant ads).

Cookies make websites smoother, more personal, and smarter by helping site owners understand what users need and what can be improved.
While generally safe, they can raise privacy concerns, which is why you’re often asked to accept or reject them.
Managing your cookies gives you more control over your online experience—balancing convenience with privacy.


But cookies aren’t just sweet digital treats—they can also be sneaky. Some hackers exploit what's called “session hijacking,” where they steal cookies to pretend they're you.
This lets them slip past security like a ninja and access accounts without needing your password again. Ever heard of “supercookies”? They’re like the overachievers of the cookie world—harder to delete, harder to escape, and sometimes used to track you across multiple websites.


Even creepier? Cookies can be used to reconstruct your digital behavior in ways most users never expect. For example, third-party cookies can reveal what sites you’ve visited, what you’ve clicked on, and even how long you stared at a product image. All of this data gets passed around for targeted advertising and analytics.
It's like your online self leaves behind digital footprints... and advertisers are out here reading them like detectives.
